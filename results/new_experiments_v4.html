Users: organizations and likely bots left out

Organizations and likely bots. The profiles of these users (bio, follower, tweet and like counts and account age, as collected) were screened: 21 are organizations (an organization keyword search of bios and handles, every match read by hand) and 3 more possibly are; 11 post more than 144 tweets a day on average over the account's life; and 21 show at least two weaker bot signals (more than 72 tweets a day, following 4,900 or more accounts, followers under 5% of follows, a handle ending in six or more digits with an empty bio, no likes, an account under 90 days old). No profile looked like a clear bot, so the 56 are a cautious upper bound. They are left out of Table 9–Table 11 and Section 5.

Where the account names come from vs. how many there are

The 12 and 25 names come from the user's closed set (the entities of the other categories' lists the user follows), while the 50 come from everything the user follows. Going from 25 to 50 names therefore changes two things at once: the number of accounts and where they come from. To separate the two, both models also ranked the candidates, in the same candidate order, from names of the full follow list: 25 drawn at random (fixed seed) from the names of the user's k = 50 prompt, the first 12 of those 25 and the first 6 of those 12. A sixth closed-set condition lists the first 6 names of the closed-set k = 12 prompt. Each smaller list is nested in the larger one from the same source. The prompts list 6.0, 11.7 and 21.7 names on average from the closed set, and 6.0, 11.9 and 22.5 from the full follow list (27.9 with k = 50), so each pair of conditions differs in one factor.

  • Where the names come from matters at every size. At ranks 81–100, names from the full follow list beat closed-set names by +0.051, +0.070 and +0.084 with 6, 12 and 25 names for GPT-6 Luna, and by +0.031, +0.033 and +0.055 for Claude Haiku 5.5: the gap grows with the number of names. At ranks 1–20 it is smaller (+0.009 to +0.028). All these differences are significant except Claude Haiku 5.5 at ranks 1–20 with 6 names (p = 0.053).
  • More closed-set names add little; more names from the full list help up to 25. From the closed set, 6 → 12 names adds +0.003 to +0.016 MAP and 12 → 25 adds +0.000 to +0.008 (the k = 25 list of 33% of users has fewer than 25 names, as they follow fewer closed-set entities). From the full list, 6 → 12 adds +0.010 to +0.032, 12 → 25 adds +0.006 to +0.022, and 25 → 50 only +0.002 to +0.005.
  • So the jump from 25 to 50 names is mostly the change of source: 97% of GPT-6 Luna's gain at ranks 81–100 (0.393 → 0.479) and 95% of Claude Haiku 5.5's; 83% and 84% at ranks 1–20.

For the 38% of users whose k = 50 prompt lists 25 names or fewer (accounts without a known name are dropped), the 25 names from the 50 are the k = 50 list itself, which is one reason the last step is small.
